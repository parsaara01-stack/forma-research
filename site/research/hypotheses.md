# Proposed hypotheses — v0.2 (unfitted; not externally preregistered)

Every proposed hypothesis needs an *ex-ante* definition of exposure, comparator, outcome, horizon, controls, minimum effect of interest and failure criterion before examining test-period returns. Below are intended specifications only.

## H01 — Capital conversion gap
- Prediction: Conditional on ex-ante growth opportunity and sector, revenue growth requiring persistent net external funding produces lower subsequent growth in free cash flow per share than growth supported by operating cash generation.
- Exposure: net equity issuance + net debt issuance, scaled by prior assets and adjusted for acquisitions.
- Comparators: matched profitability, sales growth, age, size, industry, investment and valuation.
- Outcomes: 2-year FCF/share growth, dilution-adjusted shareholder return, survival.
- Main alternative: external issuance is rational financing for high-NPV options, especially biotech and young software.

## H02 — Refinancing cliff
- Prediction: A rising refinancing requirement during a credit tightening is associated with greater investment contraction and failure probabilities.
- Exposure: committed debt principal maturing in 12–24 months / available liquidity; floating-rate exposure; refinancing spreads.
- Competing hypothesis: firms anticipate maturities and hold precautionary cash; refinancing risks may already be priced.
- Outcomes: capex cuts, covenant events, distress, return relative to matched lower-exposure companies.
- Causal caution: instrument maturity choices may be endogenous; an event-study or difference-in-differences design needs credible pre-trends and shock exogeneity.

## H03 — Incremental invested-capital efficiency
- Prediction: incremental after-tax operating profit generated per dollar of net operating investment predicts sustainable growth better than either headline sales growth or trailing ROIC.
- Exposure: rolling 3-year incremental NOPAT / rolling 3-year change in invested capital (winsorized, no zero/negative denominator abuse).
- Compare against: fixed-asset turnover, trailing ROIC, size, margins, momentum, industry-year benchmarks.
- Caveat: intangible investment is often expensed; capitalized R&D and lease-adjusted versions must be sensitivity tests.

## H04 — Recovery illusion
- Prediction: among firms forecast to increase EPS sharply, low cash conversion and high refinancing dependence predict larger future EPS forecast errors and weaker recovery persistence.
- Exposure: forecast growth measured from historic vendor vintage; financial quality known before vintage date.
- Outcome: consensus error using the same definition of GAAP vs adjusted EPS; two-year operating-income durability.
- Falsifier: no incremental improvement over analyst dispersion, sector, trend and standard accounting quality.

## H05 — Dilution and survival options
- Prediction: dilution's relationship to shareholder welfare is nonlinear and depends on capital productivity and financing runway.
- Exposure: seasoned equity issuance, compensation-related issuance, convertibles and warrants, separated.
- Outcomes: per-original-share FCF, total returns and probability of continued operations.
- Important counter: dilution can prevent otherwise value-destroying insolvency.

## H06 — Index composition illusion
- Prediction: changing-constituent aggregate EPS growth differs materially from fixed-cohort weighted earnings growth around index recompositions.
- Exposure: point-in-time members and weights; annualized sum of earnings or loss-inclusive aggregate method defined in advance.
- Separate effects: survivor improvements, disappearing losses, entrants' earnings, share-count changes and sector weights.
- No automatically meaningful single index P/E exists when aggregate earnings are negative; report profitable-only multiples separately.
